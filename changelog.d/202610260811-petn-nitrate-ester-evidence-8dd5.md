### Phase B: an evidence hunt on PETN and the nitrate esters (benchmark records, nothing scored)
- `benchmarks/energetics_props/PETN_EVIDENCE.md` and `PETN_EVIDENCE.json` (134 rows) inventory what exists for PETN and its family against the preregistration's R1 and R2 conditions, with seven primary papers read on the page: PETN has no measured gas-phase enthalpy (derived -388 kJ/mol, W1-F12 -395.5), pentaerythritol is the one four-arm compound with measured data and does not meet R1 as drafted, the R1 pattern is also matched by a hydrocarbon, four printed enthalpies are one Cheetah input table and one of them (-371) is contradicted by 171 kJ/mol. A COD pass outside the census window (`cod_nitrate_ester_pass.py`, `cod_nitrate_extra.json`) finds 73 nitrate-ester entries, 41 of them the census could not see. The fit tables of the held papers were mined (they repeat NIST, so NIST holds at least 26 nitrate esters, the closest measured neighbours of PETN being solid-phase only; Cawkwell 2021 adds gas-phase nitroglycerin). Klapoetke's 4th edition, Viswanath, Akhavan's nitroglycerin table and Boddu were read too and add no independent value. Chavez 2008 and Barone 1990 were read, and the nitramine stratum (R2) was counted: 14 nitramines in NIST (12 solid, 2 verified by the preregistration's agreement rule, 8 eligible for a holdout) and about 17 more names in Klapoetke's Encyclopedia, against about 150 verified entities needed; Keshavarz and Sadeghi's experimental column is the NIST WebBook. The eleven primaries behind NIST's nitramine rows were read: five of the eight eligible solid nitramines now rest on a primary page, and NIST's rows turn out to be re-evaluations that differ from the papers' printed figures by up to 15 kJ/mol. A 2009 recommended set (Miroshnichenko et al.) adds six measured solid nitramines and shows methyldinitramine is a liquid, so about 10 of about 13 eligible solid nitramines are now verified, still far short of the 150 R2 needs. Pedley 1986 confirms, on readable pages, the Medard-Thomas digits for pentaerythritol, EDNA, DINA, nitroguanidine, trimethylolpropane trinitrate and dipentaerythritol hexanitrate (same lineage, so no independent root). Stegeman's 1945 OSRD report (found on archive.org) is a primary for nitroguanidine, HMX, DINA and tetryl; its HMX value is an outlier. Cox and Pilcher (1970) was read from its scanned data tables: it prints the Medard-Thomas digits with decodable reference codes, shows that its nitroguanidine value is a National Bureau of Standards measurement (McEwan and Rigg 1951, [DOI in the maintainer's library]), and gives PETN a derived gas-phase enthalpy of -387.0 kJ/mol. Its N6 block holds dipentaerythritol hexanitrate (-979.5 kJ/mol, Medard and Thomas 1956); McEwan and Rigg's 1951 paper only quotes the NBS nitroguanidine value (Prosen and Gilmont, OSRD 1590, 1943); the Bradley-Cleasby file fetched is Part I (oxamide), the pentaerythritol paper is Bradley and Cotson (Part II), now read: its 143.9 kJ/mol, Nitta's 131.4 and Barone's 161.0 are second-law slopes over different temperature ranges that rise with temperature. Edwards 1953 and Cundall 1978 (the sublimation primaries for PETN and, new here, RDX, HMX, tetryl and nitroguanidine) were read. Values are quoted as facts with locators. No number of the preregistration changed and no method was scored. The 2023 Rzhevskiy paper and its Supporting Information (row G42) give a computed -402 kJ/mol for nitroisobutylglycerol trinitrate, 176 kJ/mol from the -226 that NIST prints from Medard and Thomas 1949; unresolved. Muravyev 2021 and its SI (rows G43-G51) add a measured-sublimation plus W1-F12 solid enthalpy for ten nitramines and nitramides (seven within 10 kJ/mol of the nearest experimental value, three not), DSC melting and fusion data, the SI's calculated-against-'experiment' gas-phase comparison with two internal inconsistencies, the paper's own accuracy statements (up to 25 kJ/mol) and the sublimation rule behind the Rzhevskiy numbers; the nitramine stratum stays far below R2's size. Meyer's 2016 *Explosives* (rows G52-G54) prints nitroisobutylglycerol trinitrate (-228.2 kJ/mol) and nitrourea (-282.5), agreeing with NIST but being a compilation of the same Medard-era data, and disagrees with it on DINA, metriol trinitrate and mannitol hexanitrate by 25 to 33 kJ/mol. The whole book is mined (rows G55-G57; the parsed corpus stays in private notes): 214 entries, 13 nitramines and 21 nitrate esters with a printed enthalpy, all unlabelled. The Army Encyclopedia (rows G58-G60) holds nitrourea (-281.2 kJ/mol) and an early combustion value (unstated source) for nitroisobutylglycerol trinitrate that, read as kcal/kg, gives about -403 kJ/mol (-375 bias-scaled), supporting the computed -402 and contradicting the -226 of NIST and Meyer; the Memorial de l'artillerie francaise index (G61) confirms Medard's tables as vol. 28 no. 2 p. 415.
