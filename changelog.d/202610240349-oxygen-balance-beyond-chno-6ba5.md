### Oxygen balance beyond C/H/N/O

- The Oxygen Balance calculator now computes structures containing sulfur, chlorine and
  thirteen metals (Al, Li, Na, K, Ca, Sr, Ba, Pb, Zn, Cr, Co, Hg, Ag) on the CO₂ basis,
  with each element burned to the product used by Meyer, Köhler & Homburg's *Explosives*
  (7th ed.): sulfur to SO₂; chlorine to HCl or the metal's chloride (the number is the same
  either way); each metal to the oxide the book's printed balances use, with mercury and
  silver left as the metal. 35 of the book's printed balances are tests, as are three rows of
  Akhavan's *The Chemistry of Explosives*. Every assumed product is named in the result.
- Mercury is the one product the two books disagree on (Meyer leaves the metal, Akhavan
  burns it to HgO). The result follows Meyer and gives the HgO figure beside it.
- Still refused, by name: fluorine, bromine, iodine, boron, phosphorus, magnesium, copper,
  iron (the book uses two different oxides for it), every other element, and more chlorine
  than the structure's hydrogen and metals can take. The CO basis is still reported for
  C/H/N/O only, and C/H/N/O results are unchanged.
- Kamlet–Jacobs detonation stays C/H/N/O only: all four Kamlet papers and Meyer's entry
  state it for C-H-N-O explosives.
