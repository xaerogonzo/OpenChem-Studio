### A drawn ClF3 (BrF3, ClF5) is now the molecule; calculators say why they have nothing for it
- **Drawing chlorine trifluoride makes the molecule ClF3**: the editor, undo and redo, duplicate, "use this conformer in 2D", the conformer service and the ORCA panel's charge default hold structures OpenChem's hypervalent table accepts and RDKit cannot sanitise. Conformer generation gives the T-shape (F-Cl-F 87.45 and 174.9 degrees), labelled as an unoptimised VSEPR geometry.
- **Everything that needs an RDKit molecule refuses, once, at the shared input step**, with the new limit code `HYPERVALENT_UNSUPPORTED` (kind LIMIT): a calculator result says "Not available for this hypervalent structure", and the always-on properties report the same sentence, with no traceback in the log. Measured on ClF3 first: Gasteiger charges came out NaN and Crippen logP and TPSA returned numbers that mean nothing, which is why nothing is allowed through by default. Five always-on properties are plain arithmetic on the atoms and stay available on ClF3 (molecular weight 92.447, exact mass 91.964, formula ClF3, formal charge, heavy-atom count); everything else is refused. An IF3-style structure with no measured geometry now fails conformer generation with a stated reason and a warning, not a traceback.
- A real-window test (`tests/test_hypervalent_window.py`) holds the molecule to this, and fails on an exception inside a Qt slot as well as on a logged error, because the first version of the change had one in the ORCA panel that logging alone did not show.
