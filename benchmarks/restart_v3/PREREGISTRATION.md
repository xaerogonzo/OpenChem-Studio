# Numerical frequencies for the radicals an analytic Hessian cannot be trusted on (protocol v3), and a halogen arm: preregistration

**Status: DRAFT, written 2026-10-26; decisions D1 to D5 taken by Alex on 2026-10-27 (section 9). NOT FROZEN. Arms C, C-Br and D have not been computed, and nothing in this file changes a verdict that was already given.** Section 10 lists what must happen before it is frozen. A calibration that was run on spent data is reported in section 5. The literature behind sections 6 and 7 is recorded in `LITERATURE_LOG.md`, and the numbers derived from it are in `references.json`, recomputed by `tests/test_restart_v3_references.py`.

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

The bond-dissociation benchmarks (`benchmarks/bde/PREREGISTRATION.md`, PARTIAL; `benchmarks/restart_v2/PREREGISTRATION.md`, held-out arm PASS, stress arm DO NOT ADOPT) both lost the S-H bond of the thiyl radicals to an imaginary mode that no restart removed. **Their verdicts stand and nothing here re-judges them.** This asks a narrower question that the probe in `benchmarks/thiyl_probe/` opened: is the imaginary mode real, and if it is not, can the calculator get a value for those bonds without a new kind of risk?

It is **not** a way to improve a score: Arms C and D are sets of bonds nobody has computed (Arm D's references share an anchor with a spent bond, section 7), Arm C-Br is reported and decides nothing, Arms A and B are made on bonds whose results are spent and are labelled so.

## 3. The v3 rule (everything here is fixed once frozen)

v3 is v2 (which is v1 plus the displaced restart) **plus one additional stage for a RADICAL (a doublet) at its accepted or final geometry**. Nothing changes for a closed-shell structure.

* **R1, the imaginary-mode trigger (fixed now).** A radical whose lowest analytic frequency is still below -20 cm-1 after v1's `Calc_Hess` re-run, and whose skeleton is intact, gets ONE numerical frequency job at **the geometry of that re-run**: `! M062X def2-TZVPD TightSCF DefGrid3 NumFreq` (same functional, basis and grid; `%pal nprocs 4`, `%maxcore 3000`; charge 0, multiplicity 2, unrestricted). v2's displaced restart is **not** applied to it first: the probe shows there is no saddle to leave.
  * **Accepted** when the numerical lowest frequency is >= -20 cm-1. Its enthalpy is that job's own `Total enthalpy` (298.15 K, 1 atm), at the same electronic energy.
  * **Below -20 cm-1 (decision D2, 2026-10-27, replacing the draft's displaced restart):** the numerical job's enthalpy is **kept** and the radical is reported **"frequencies not verified: a soft mode below -20 cm-1 remains"**. The displaced restart is not applied: the numerical Hessian is not obviously the better answer for a floppy radical (the acetyloxyl and acetyl rows of section 5 have numerical modes at -95 and -30 cm-1), and the restart made the thiyl radical worse where there was no saddle to leave (section 2, item 1).
* **R2, the stability trigger (decided mechanically after section 5, never by eye).** Every radical gets a stability analysis (`%scf StabPerform true end`, a single point at its final geometry, 15 seconds to 4 minutes) and the smallest eigenvalue `e` is recorded. If section 5's rule (below) found a threshold `T`, a radical with `e < T` gets the same numerical job as R1 whether or not an imaginary mode appeared, and its enthalpy is the numerical one. **If section 5 did not find a clean threshold, R2 is not part of v3, and a radical with `e` below the lowest eigenvalue seen in a radical that agreed with its numerical Hessian is reported with the warning "the analytic frequencies of this radical were not verified" and its value is kept.** The step is a trigger and an arbiter, never a way to drop a value. **Resolved by section 5 (2026-10-27): no threshold exists, R2 is not part of v3, and the fallback flags nothing.**
* **The cost cap (decision D1, section 9: 12 atoms, taken 2026-10-27).** A numerical Hessian is 6N gradients, which is hours for a radical of more than about 12 atoms. v3 as preregistered applies R1 and R2 to radicals of **at most 12 atoms**; a larger one is not given the numerical job and is reported "analytic frequencies not verified". The calibration measured radicals of at most 9 atoms only (section 5), so **v3 makes no claim about radicals of 10 to 12 atoms beyond what the 9-atom ones suggest.**
* **Records, provenance.** The numerical job writes the v2 record fields plus `stage` (`numerical`), `numerical`, `verified`, the `label` when its frequencies do not pass, and `analytic_enthalpy` / `analytic_lowest_frequency` beside `enthalpy`, so the effect of the replacement is always readable. (The draft also promised a `stability` stage and a smallest stability eigenvalue; R2 is not part of v3, so neither is written.) **Provenance:** every record is stamped with the commit and whether the watched folders (`src`, `benchmarks/restart_v3`, `benchmarks/thiyl_probe`, `benchmarks/restart_v2`, `benchmarks/bde`, `benchmarks/bde_heldout`, `benchmarks/proton_affinity`; their `artifacts/` excluded) were dirty AT THE MOMENT IT IS WRITTEN, not once per run. A report is refused (NOT EVALUABLE) for records from more than one commit, records with no commit, records made with a dirty tree, **and records whose commit differs from HEAD in any watched folder** (references or runner edited after the run), and for an arm with an unclosed chain. A numerical job that fails or times out (4 hours, the first run's limit) is a missing structure with its reason; a job that could not be STARTED leaves the chain open to be redone. The report names every numerical job that gave no result, and for each bond which structures its value came from (lowest frequencies, and whether the radical was analytic or numerical).

## 5. Arm A: the calibration (run 2026-10-26 on the radicals both benchmarks accepted)

**What was run.** `benchmarks/thiyl_probe/calibrate.py` (stability analysis of all **65** accepted radicals of the first run and the held-out arm; analytic and numerical frequencies for the **41** its selection rule picked, which excludes the **15** radicals of 10 to 14 atoms: the secondary and tertiary alkyl, n-propyl and n-butyl, the propoxyls and tert-butoxyl, phenyl, benzyl, phenoxyl, benzoyloxyl and tert-butylperoxyl radicals). The full table is `benchmarks/thiyl_probe/calibration/calibration_table.json`, with `stability.json`, `numfreq.json`, `selected.json`, `radicals.json` and the ORCA inputs of the radicals named below. ORCA's own output files are kept outside Git (their banner trips the repository's product-name guard); the numbers read from them are in the JSON.

**One selected radical produced no measurement and is not in the table.** Methyl hydroperoxide's C-H radical (`heldout_methyl_hydroperoxide_C_H_Hydroperoxymethyl`) crashed in ORCA's coupled-perturbed SCF step (`orca_scfresp_mpi`, exit code 0xc0000409, "Failed to write HEADER of job.cpscfvec"), and was rerun once, with the same result. Its numerical job started and stalled after its first displaced SCF (no file written for 8 hours until it was killed). It is listed in `unmeasured.json` and counted below as unmeasured, not as non-material. So the table has **40** radicals, not 41.

**The table, read by the rule below (2026-10-27).** Of the 40 measured radicals, **4 are material and 36 are not.** The material four:

| radical | atoms | smallest stability eigenvalue `e` (Eh) | enthalpy shift (kcal/mol, numerical minus analytic) | largest mode difference (cm-1) | why material |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| methoxy (methanol O-H) | 5 | 0.00487 | +0.272 | 208.4 | both |
| ethoxyl (stress set) | 8 | 0.00611 | +0.175 | 101.6 | mode |
| acetyloxyl (acetic acid O-H) | 7 | 0.02587 | -0.638 | 113.1 | both |
| acetyl (acetaldehyde formyl C-H) | 6 | 0.08728 | -0.658 | 136.4 | both |

* The largest material `e` is **0.0873** (acetyl). The smallest non-material `e` is **-0.00149** (the SH radical of hydrogen sulfide), and **18 of the 36** non-material radicals have `e` at or below 0.0873. Four non-material radicals have a smaller `e` than any material one (the SH radical -0.00149, the OH radical of water 0.00018, azide 0.00130 and the cyanooxidanyl radical 0.00211).
* **The two groups overlap, so by step 3 of the rule `T` does not exist and R2 is not in v3.** The smallest stability eigenvalue does not predict which radicals carry an analytic error. The material four are not even among the lowest `e`: the largest error in the table (acetyl, -0.66 kcal/mol) belongs to a radical whose `e` is 0.087, higher than that of half the radicals with none.
* Under section 3's fallback sentence (a radical with `e` below the lowest `e` of a radical that AGREED with its numerical Hessian is flagged "not verified"), the lowest such `e` is -0.00149, which is the lowest in the table, so **the fallback flags nothing**: R2 contributes neither a trigger nor a warning, and the answer to "when are the analytic frequencies not trustworthy" rests on R1 alone.
* **The methanethiyl and ethanethiyl radicals are not rows of this table**, because the benchmarks that produced it refused them (the imaginary mode). Their `e` (0.0005 and 0.0015, `benchmarks/thiyl_probe/artifacts/`) are within the range of non-material rows (water, azide), so adding them as material rows would change nothing about the overlap.
* **What R1 alone misses**: the material four are radicals with no imaginary mode after the re-run, so R1 would not have given any of them a numerical job. That is 4 of 40 (10%) measured radicals with an analytic enthalpy off by 0.17 to 0.66 kcal/mol; the held-out arm's standard error was 0.25. This is the cost of keeping R1 and no R2, stated here so it is not discovered later.
* **A caveat on R1's acceptance threshold (-20 cm-1)**: for the acetyloxyl and acetyl radicals the *numerical* lowest frequency is itself below it (-95.2 and -29.7 cm-1, against +17.9 and +106.7 analytic). Both are floppy radicals, and a numerical Hessian on a soft torsion is not obviously the better answer. Under R1 as written such a result would be "not accepted" and sent to v2's displaced restart. This is a property of the rule as drafted, not something the calibration decided, and it is part of what Alex is asked in D2 (section 9).
* **Sample sizes**: 40 radicals (4 material, 36 not), of at most 9 atoms; none of 10 to 14 atoms was measured (15 radicals), so nothing here speaks to them.

**The rule that reads the table, fixed before the table was read.**
1. A radical is **material** when the absolute analytic-to-numerical enthalpy shift is at least **0.25 kcal/mol** (the standard error of the mean error of the held-out arm; a shift below it cannot be told from noise in a verdict) **or** its largest mode difference is at least **100 cm-1**.
2. If every material radical has a smaller `e` than every non-material one, `T` is the **geometric mean** of the largest material `e` and the smallest non-material `e`, rounded to one significant figure. R2 is in v3 with that `T`.
3. If the two groups overlap, **R2 is not in v3** (section 3).
4. The table, `T` or the statement that none exists, and how many radicals of each kind there were are written into this section before the freeze, with the sample sizes. **A threshold from fewer than three material radicals is stated as resting on that few.**

## 6. Arm C: the references

The six bonds, from `benchmarks/bde_heldout/candidates_halogen.json` (ATcT 1.228, kcal/mol, with the table's uncertainty): chloromethane C-H to chloromethyl **99.41 +- 0.21**; dichloromethane C-H to dichloromethyl **96.20 +- 0.23**; chloroform C-H to trichloromethyl **93.31 +- 0.22**; fluoroform C-H to trifluoromethyl **106.67 +- 0.14**; formyl fluoride C-H to fluoroformyl **101.38 +- 0.12**; formyl chloride C-H to chloroformyl **90.95 +- 0.20**. The rule that chose them is in `extract_atct_halogen.py`; `tests/test_bde_heldout_halogen.py` fails if a seventh bond appears, if one overlaps the first run or the first held-out arm, or if one is not a C-H bond of H, C, O, F and Cl.

**What the table does not hold, and so what Arm C cannot test:** no fluoromethyl, difluoromethyl, bromomethyl or tribromomethyl radical, and no chloroethyl, halovinyl or halophenyl radical; **bromine is therefore not tested at all**, and nothing here supports a claim about a C-H bond outside a halomethane or a formyl halide.


**The bromine bonds of Arm C-Br, from the literature and NOT from ATcT (read 2026-10-27; REPORTED ONLY, decision D5).** The saved ATcT table holds 44 gas-phase species with carbon and bromine and no bromo radical of the kind needed, which is why the six bonds above have none. Three papers by one laboratory measured the C-H bond energy directly, from the forward and reverse kinetics of R + HBr = RH + Br in a photoionization mass-spectrometer reactor (second-law; the reverse-reaction kinetics were taken from earlier work):

| bond | C-H bond energy (kJ/mol) | in kcal/mol | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| bromomethane to bromomethyl | 427.2 +- 2.4 | 102.10 +- 0.57 | Seetula 2003 (`10.1039/b210787m`) |
| bromochloromethane to bromochloromethyl | 406.0 +- 2.4 | 97.04 +- 0.57 | Seetula 2003 |
| dibromomethane to dibromomethyl | 412.6 +- 2.7 | 98.61 +- 0.65 | Seetula and Eskola 2008 (`10.1016/j.chemphys.2008.04.008`) |
| bromoethane (alpha C-H) to 1-bromoethyl | 415.0 +- 2.7 | 99.19 +- 0.65 | Seetula and Eskola 2008 |

All four are inside the 1.0 kcal/mol rule for a deciding bond. What reading them showed, and what it does not show:
* **They are one laboratory's kinetic measurements, not an evaluated value.** ATcT combines many determinations; these four rest on one method and one apparatus. The bond energy is the measured reaction enthalpy plus the H-Br bond energy, so it does not depend on the parent molecule's formation enthalpy (the radical's formation enthalpy does).
* **The 2008 value for the dibromomethyl radical (199.1 +- 2.7 kJ/mol) agrees with the calculations of three theoretical studies** quoted in that paper (196.9 to 201.3) and with a recalculation of an earlier experiment (198.9), and it supersedes the same author's 2000 ab initio value (180 +- 5), which the 2000 paper itself warns is low-accuracy for radicals with several bromine atoms. A fourth theoretical study (191.7 +- 3.0) is lower.
* **Tribromomethyl has no usable value**: only the 2000 ab initio estimate (195 +- 5, plus 2.4 for the method), which fails the 1.0 kcal/mol rule on its own uncertainty, so it is excluded.
* **The calculator's scope does not include bromine**, and nothing here tests heavy-atom effects (spin-orbit coupling, scalar relativity) that M06-2X/def2-TZVPD ignores or treats approximately. A pass would show the unmodified protocol gives an acceptable error on four bromo C-H bonds against these references; it would not show the references are right.

**What the wider sweep of 2026-10-27 added (`references.json`, in kJ/mol; the other determinations are Luo's Table 3.5; "calculated" is Andress 2026 Table 2, Feller-Peterson-Dixon / G3(MP2), no uncertainty stated):**

| bond | kinetics (Seetula) | other determinations (value +- uncertainty) | calculated |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH3Br | 427.2 +- 2.4 | **425.1 +- 4.2 (bromination, an independent method)**; 426.8 +- 8.4; 421.7 +- 8.4 | 420.5 / 420.8 |
| CH2Br2 | 412.6 +- 2.7 | 417.1 +- 7.5; 409.6 +- 8.4; 411 (review); 433.9 +- 8.4 (1958 thermal cracking, an old outlier) | 407.4 / 406.5 |
| CH2BrCl | 406.0 +- 2.4 | **393 (a review, no uncertainty printed)** | 403.8 / 404.4 |
| CH3CH2Br alpha | 415.0 +- 2.7 | 406.7 +- 4.2 (photobromination); 403.3; 394.1 +- 8.6 | not covered |

* **Bromomethane is the best-supported bond**: an independent bromination determination agrees with the kinetics value inside the combined uncertainty (the bromination value is the lower, by 2.1 kJ/mol).
* **Two are contested.** Bromoethane's kinetics value is the highest of the determinations, about 2 kcal/mol above the best of the others (8.3 kJ/mol against a combined 5.0); bromochloromethane's disagrees with a review by 13 kJ/mol, though that review prints no uncertainty.
* **The calculation sits below the kinetics value for all three bonds it covers, by 6.7, 5.2 and 2.2 kJ/mol.** On the three bonds whose reference is ATcT (CH4, CHF3, CH2Cl2) the same calculation sits 2.5, 1.0 and 2.7 kJ/mol below, so part of the gap is the calculation's own offset; about 4 kJ/mol (1 kcal/mol) is not explained by it for bromomethane and about 2.5 for dibromomethane (against Seetula 2008). It is suggestive, not proof: the calculation has no stated uncertainty on these bonds, and a heavy-atom (spin-orbit) effect is possible.
* **The kinetics method is disputed** (Leplat 2013): two incompatible sets of R + HBr rate data give ethyl formation enthalpies of 120.7 (Seetula) and 117.3 +- 3.1 kJ/mol (this paper, third law), and the paper's own second-law result fails an entropy check. It tested ethyl only, not a bromoalkyl radical; the direction (a higher radical enthalpy, hence a higher bond energy) matches the calculation's, but nothing here shows the bromine values are biased.
* **So the references' true uncertainty is wider than printed, probably by about 1 kcal/mol, and that is why Arm C-Br decides nothing.** Tribromomethane is excluded: no reference passes the 1.0 kcal/mol rule (Luo: 401.7 +- 6.7 kJ/mol).

## 7. Arm D: the S-H references (the section this one replaces said there could be none)

**What was not available.**
* ATcT 1.228 (the newest version listed) has no CH3S or CH3CH2S: the thiyl radicals it holds are SH (hydrogen sulfide's, the first run's) and the inorganic HSO and HOS from HSOH and H2SO.
* Luo's handbook (Table 8.1) names a recommended value only for H-SH (91.2) and CH3S-H (87.4 +- 0.5), both in the first run. Ethanethiol's values are 88.6 +- 2, 87.3 and 87.5 (the last two derived or correlated, with no uncertainty given); benzenethiol's range from 79.4 to 83.5 and none is recommended.
**What was found (2026-10-27): the gas-phase acidity route.** A thiol's S-H bond energy is `D0(RS-H) = acidity(0 K) + EA(RS) - IE(H)`; Ervin 2015 re-anchored the acidity scale (H2S, CH3SH, pyrrole) by a least-squares network over proton-transfer equilibria and gives each acid's absolute acidity at about the 95% level; the electron affinities are from laser photodetachment. The three bonds of Arm D, with the arithmetic recomputed by `tests/test_restart_v3_references.py`:

| bond | acidity dH0 (kJ/mol) | electron affinity (eV) | D0 (kJ/mol) | D298 (kcal/mol) | status of D298 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ethanethiol S-H (radical of 8 atoms) | 1483.6 +- 1.7 | 1.947 +- 0.013 (Moran and Ellison 1988) | 359.5 +- 2.1 (published) | **87.16 +- 0.53** | **published** (Ervin 2015) |
| 1-propanethiol S-H (11 atoms) | 1481.0 +- 1.6 | 2.00 +- 0.02 (Janousek 1980) | 361.9 +- 2.5 | **87.72 +- 0.62** | **derived here** (acidity cycle plus a computed thermal step of 5.09 kJ/mol), not published |
| 2-propanethiol S-H (11 atoms) | 1476.5 +- 1.7 | 2.02 +- 0.02 (Janousek 1980) | 359.4 +- 2.6 | **87.08 +- 0.63** | **derived here** (acidity cycle plus a computed thermal step of 4.98 kJ/mol), not published |

* **The D298 of the two propanethiols is my arithmetic, not a published number.** The step from 0 K to 298 K adds the radical's and the hydrogen atom's thermal enthalpies and subtracts the parent's. Ervin published it for ethanethiol only (+5.2 kJ/mol); for the propanethiols it is **computed** (`thermal_step.py`, records in `artifacts/thermal_step/`): B3LYP/def2-TZVPD harmonic frequencies of every kept conformer, zero-point energy excluded, taken from the lowest-enthalpy conformer that is a minimum of its species. **The method is held to the one published step: it gives 5.16 kJ/mol for ethanethiol against Ervin's 5.2.** The propanethiols' steps are **5.09 kJ/mol (1-propanethiol; spread over its parent/radical conformer combinations 0.74)** and **4.98 kJ/mol (2-propanethiol; 0.06, but see the next point)**; the uncertainty carried is the larger of 0.5 kJ/mol and that spread (the range over every usable radical conformer against every usable parent conformer), added in quadrature to the 0 K value's. The step is a property of the reference (it converts a published 0 K bond energy to 298 K), not of the calculation under test, and it moved the two references by -0.02 and -0.05 kcal/mol from the borrowed 5.2.
* **A caution found while computing it, and what following it showed (2026-10-28; `artifacts/saddle_follow/summary.json`).** Two of the three 2-propanethiyl structures optimised by B3LYP have a frequency of -233 cm-1 and an ENTHALPY 0.54 kcal/mol lower than the third, a minimum. (An earlier version of this text, and of the pull request that carried it, said 2.3 kcal/mol; that was an arithmetic slip, 0.00086 hartree is 0.54 kcal/mol.) The script rejects them as it rejects any structure with a frequency below -20 cm-1, so the step comes from the minimum. **The imaginary mode is a very flat double well, not a lower state:** displaced +-0.15 Angstrom along it and re-optimised, both ends reach minima (lowest frequency +216 cm-1) whose ELECTRONIC energy is within 0.01 kcal/mol of the minimum already found (conformer 0), and the saddle point lies only 0.04 kcal/mol above them; the saddle looks lower in enthalpy only because the harmonic treatment drops its imaginary mode from the zero-point and thermal terms. So no deeper state was missed, the step stands (the minima's thermal enthalpies agree with conformer 0's to 0.13 kJ/mol), and the finding is a warning about the benchmark itself: **if M06-2X leaves a radical on such a flat saddle, R1's numerical job at the SAME geometry reproduces the saddle and its enthalpy is about 0.5 kcal/mol too low, which lowers the bond energy by that much (the calculation already runs low).** The result is labelled "frequencies not verified" (D2) and the report prints the lowest frequencies, so it is visible; it is not corrected. The follow-up used B3LYP only: the benchmark's M06-2X protocol was deliberately NOT run on any Arm D bond, because that would look at a held-out result before the freeze.
* **A third methanethiol measurement exists that Ervin did not use (read 2026-10-28).** Ervin's anchor D0(CH3S-H) = 359.7 +- 1.5 kJ/mol combines two routes: the Br + CH3SH kinetics of Nicovich 1992 and a photofragment route through D0(CH3-S). A 2025 paper on methanethiol photodissociation (`wu2025`, which has Ashfold as an author) quotes **D0(H3CS-H) = 30 250 +- 100 cm-1 = 361.87 +- 1.20 kJ/mol** from Wilson, Ashfold and Dixon, J. Chem. Phys. 101, 7538 (1994), an H-atom photofragment translational-spectroscopy measurement of the S-H bond itself (that paper has NOT been read; only its citation in `wu2025` has). It is **2.17 kJ/mol (0.52 kcal/mol) above Ervin's anchor**, **1.13 times the combined uncertainty (1.92): outside one of them, inside two** (Ervin's own is about the 95% level and Wilson et al.'s is not stated as one, so the two are not on the same footing); the inverse-variance mean of the two is 361.03 +- 0.94, a shift of +1.33 kJ/mol (+0.32 kcal/mol). Because the thiols' acidities are measured relative to methanethiol, all three Arm D references could rise by up to that much (an upper bound: the least-squares network with the H2S and pyrrole anchors passes less), which would make the calculation's error more NEGATIVE by up to 0.3 kcal/mol; adopting the new value alone would be up to 0.5. **This is a sensitivity, not a correction: the references stay as Ervin computed them, and the report of Arm D must state it.** (`references.json` `methanethiol_anchor`, recomputed by the references test.)
* **The references are not independent of each other, or of the first run's methanethiol bond.** All come from one acidity network with the same anchors, and the CH3SH anchor is the Nicovich 1992 kinetics value the first run used. For the standard error the arm is therefore treated as **n = 3 with correlated errors**, and the report says so. The ethanethiol electron affinity is a separate measurement (Moran and Ellison 1988 and Janousek 1980 agree: 1.947 +- 0.013 and 1.953 +- 0.006 eV).
* **Left out:** 2-methyl-2-propanethiol (its radical has 14 atoms, over the 12-atom cap of D1; its reference, about 87.4 +- 0.66, is as good as the others) and **thiophenol**, whose determinations span about 5 kcal/mol (Borges dos Santos 2002 83.5 +- 1.1 from solution calorimetry; Lim 2007 D0 76.8 +- 0.3 at 0 K; Luo's Table 8.1 79.4 to 83.5, none recommended). 2-Thiophenethiol and benzyl mercaptan have no reference found.
* **Methanethiol's S-H** (Nicovich 1992: 87.35 +- 0.58 kcal/mol at 298 K) is the first run's lost bond. It is computed with v3 and reported **outside the verdict**, because the calculator's S-H value rests on it and because it is spent.
* **What Arm D can and cannot support.** If it passes, the calculator's S-H value for a thiol may be shown with "validated against three held-out S-H references of about 0.6 kcal/mol, which share an anchor". It does not support a claim about aromatic or heteroaryl thiols, or about thiyl radicals of more than 12 atoms.

## 8. The reading rules (Arms C and D: unchanged from the held-out arm, `benchmarks/bde/judge.py` is not edited; Arm C-Br: no verdict)

On the six deciding bonds: mean absolute error, mean signed error, largest absolute error, each with the standard error of the mean.
* **PASS:** MAE <= 2.5 kcal/mol, no error above 5.0, |MSE| <= 1.5. **PARTIAL:** not PASS and MAE <= 3.5. **FAIL:** otherwise. **NOT EVALUABLE:** more than one of the six has no computed value.
* **At n = 6 the standard error of the mean error is about 0.25 kcal/mol if the spread resembles the held-out arm's measured one (its standard error was 0.12 at n = 27, a standard deviation near 0.6), and about 0.5 if it is as wide as that arm's preregistration assumed; a result within 0.5 of a limit cannot tell the two sides apart and the report says so.** A PASS here is a statement about these six bonds.
* The bias test of v2 (section 7 there) is **not** applied: six bonds cannot reproduce or refute a mean of -1.5.

**Arm D (the S-H bonds)** is read by the same limits on its **three** deciding bonds, separately from Arm C (a PASS there says nothing about S-H and the reverse): PASS, PARTIAL and FAIL as above; **NOT EVALUABLE: two or three of the three have no computed value** (one missing leaves two bonds, which the report states). At n = 3 the standard error of the mean error is about 0.35 kcal/mol if the spread resembles the held-out arm's (a standard deviation near 0.6), the references are each uncertain by about 0.6 kcal/mol and **are not independent of each other** (a shared acidity anchor, section 7), so **a result within about 0.7 kcal/mol of a limit cannot tell the two sides apart and the report says so.** A PASS is a statement about these three bonds. Methanethiol's S-H is reported beside them and does not enter the mean.

**Arm C-Br (the bromine bonds) has no verdict.** For each of the four bonds the report lists the computed value and its error against (i) the kinetics value, (ii) the range of the other determinations of section 6, and (iii) the calculated value where there is one. **A claim is made only for a gross failure**: the error exceeds 5.0 kcal/mol against the kinetics value and also against EVERY calculated value (two are printed for each bond that has any: Feller-Peterson-Dixon and G3(MP2)); for bromoethane, which has none, more than 5.0 OUTSIDE THE WHOLE RANGE of the other determinations on whichever side the computed value lies (decided 2026-10-28: symmetric, so the low and the high side are treated alike). A refused report makes no claim. The text report prints the error against all three kinds of reference, not only the kinetics value. Anything else is reported as numbers and nothing is claimed: the references cannot decide a smaller disagreement (section 6).
