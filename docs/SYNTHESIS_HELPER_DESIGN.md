# Synthesis helper: design and spike record (2026-10-19)

Status: **Stage 0 (the spike) in progress; no application code; Stage 1 is not authorized until this note records a GO.** The last item of Phase 4 of the suite roadmap. The approved plan is rev 3 (kept with the session's plan file); this note is its durable record and takes the results as the spike runs.

## What it is, and what it is never claimed to be

Given a target molecule, an **optional external planner** proposes multi-step routes; OpenChem adds its own annotations to every molecule in a route. Standing rules: claims are measured, not asserted; "measured, not shipped" is an accepted outcome; **"no route found within the configured search limits" is a valid answer**; **a returned route is a hypothesis**, never a validated or feasible synthesis. A policy probability is the planner's number and not a measured chance a reaction works; a route score ranks, it does not predict feasibility; "in stock" is a match to a catalogue snapshot, not availability or legality to sell.

## Decisions (Alex, 2026-10-19)

1. **First planner to spike: AiZynthFinder.** Not committed to shipping it: if licence, Windows install or quality fails, the spike records NO-GO and names the next candidate (Syntheseus, or a small in-house RDKit/rdchiral template planner).
2. **Locate-only install**: the app never fetches anything (as Psi4). A download button is a later stage with pinned URLs and checksums, if ever.
3. **Regulated targets: routes are shown with an alert, with one exception.** No planning starts for a target matched to a **CWC Schedule 1** entry (the shipped `cwc-schedule-1` ruleset); the refusal names the reason. DEA listings, CWC Schedules 2 and 3 and everything else are alert-only.

## What was verified against the source

* **AiZynthFinder 4.4.1** (`pyproject.toml`, master, fetched 2026-10-19): Python `>=3.10,<3.13`; `numpy <2.0.0`; `onnxruntime ^1.23.0` required; TensorFlow only in the optional `tf` extra; console scripts `aizynthcli`, `aizynthapp`, `download_public_data`, `smiles2stock`, `cat_aizynth_output`. **The user's planner environment is therefore a separate Python from OpenChem's.**
* **No random-seed setting** appears in the README; the search settings include `max_transforms`, `iteration_limit`, `time_limit` (default 120 s) and `return_first`. The noise-floor design (an `iteration_limit` budget, repeated identical runs) follows.
* **In this repository:** `chem/isotopes.py` `same_structure` exists (merged); `RegulatoryEngine.screen(mol, jurisdictions, include_near_misses, as_of)` returns a `ScreeningReport` whose findings carry `ruleset_id` and `rule_id`, and the Schedule 1 ruleset id is `cwc-schedule-1`.
* **Gaps in the first plan, found by reading the code, and how the plan closes them:**
  * **The shared regulatory engine includes user rulesets** (`calculator._shared_engine()` calls `load_all()` with `include_user=True`, and `add_ruleset` honours `supersedes`), so a user file could supersede `cwc-schedule-1`. **The gate builds its own engine from `load_all(include_user=False)`** and, if `cwc-schedule-1` is missing, has load problems or screening raises, reports `unscreened` and plans nothing. The ruleset's own `known_limitations` (ricin and saxitoxin not encoded; size limits over-report and never under-report; "corresponding alkylated salts" not matched) travel with every refusal: the gate is a list match, not a guarantee.
  * **The reaction-prediction plugin cannot reach a service** (top-level `plugins/reaction_prediction/`, narrow `PluginContext` namespaces only). Stage 1 adds a narrow `context.synthesis` handle, modelled on `PluginCalculations`.
  * **Nothing draws a reaction**, and `ui/` may not import RDKit. Stage 1 adds a `chem/` reaction-SVG function.
  * The bridge's service-run class (`PREVIEW_ONLY` exists in `domain/calculation_bridge.py`) is checked again when the assistant tool is built.

### What the AiZynthFinder paper itself reports (Genheden et al., J. Cheminform. 2020, 12, 70, DOI 10.1186/s13321-020-00472-1; PMC7672904, open access, read from the full text)

* **The one benchmark it runs:** 100 randomly chosen ChEMBL compounds, the ZINC-subset stock and the (then) USPTO policy, against ASKCOS's public tree builder: **AiZynthFinder found a route for 55, ASKCOS for 62; 47 were solved by both, 8 by AiZynthFinder only, 15 by ASKCOS only, 30 by neither**; average search time 38.7 s and average time to the first solution 7.1 s; an average of 2.4 steps. A solution means "every precursor is in stock", not that the route is chemically right.
* **What it says decides whether a route is found:** the stock ("including the readily available Enamine building blocks ... an additional 10 compounds"), the template library (a Reaxys-trained policy found 56, "although there is not a complete overlap"; those policies are not public "due to licensing agreements"), and the target's complexity (the SA score is generally lower for solved compounds, which is why the SA annotation belongs on a route).
* **What it says about the route score:** "designed to support the tree search and is rather indiscriminate with regard to the quality of the route (i.e. if it's a good route or not) and should be interpreted with care." This is the paper's own warning against reading a route score as quality.
* **Not in the paper:** any comparison with reference routes, any chemist's feasibility check of the 55 routes, any statement about a random seed (**none is mentioned**), stereochemistry or conditions. The numbers are for the 2020 software and policy; the current ONNX policy and 4.4.1 are not the system measured there. So **no published accuracy for the current release was found in this paper**, and the preregistered benchmark has to supply it.

### 0c. What the planner supplies, field by field (seven real targets, 100 iterations, ZINC stock and USPTO policy and filter; raw output in `benchmarks/synthesis/fixtures/`)

* **Per route:** a tree of molecule and reaction nodes; one score, `state score` (the paper's warning applies: it ranks the search's states and is "rather indiscriminate" about route quality).
* **Per molecule node:** `smiles`, `in_stock`, `is_chemical`, `hide`. Stock status exists for every node.
* **Per reaction node:** a `smiles` that is the **template** fragment (product >> reactants, partly mapped), and a `metadata` block with **`mapped_reaction_smiles`, the whole reaction of the real molecules with atom maps and stereo (present on 104 of 104 steps seen)**, `policy_probability` and its rank, `policy_name`, `template` (retro SMARTS), `template_code`, `template_hash`, `library_occurence` and `classification` (the 0.0 "Unrecognized" in every case seen). **No conditions, no yields, no reagents or solvents.** Atoms that only the reactants carry (a leaving group, the other half of a reagent) get new map numbers on the right and nothing on the left.
* **Search statistics:** solved flag, number of routes, number of solved routes, nodes, first-solution iteration and time, search time, the precursors in and out of stock, `profiling.iterations` and the policy call counts. **There is no field that says why the search stopped**: a stop at the iteration limit has to be read from `iterations == limit`, one at the time limit from `search_time >= limit`, and anything else is `unknown`. `build_routes` returns fewer routes than the tree holds (12 of 46 for aspirin).
* **No random seed**, as the README implies. Observed run to run with identical configuration: the solved flag, the top score and the solved-route count were the same every time (racemic ibuprofen unsolved four times out of four, (S)-ibuprofen solved three of three with 28 solved routes each time), while **the tree size and route count varied** (racemic ibuprofen: 131, 144, 144 and 148 routes, 537 to 550 nodes, 14 to 18 s) and the time varied by about 20%.
* **Outcomes that look like chemistry and are not to be read as such:** aspirin from salicylic acid and acetic anhydride, paracetamol, caffeine and (S)-ibuprofen are solved in **one step** from stock; racemic ibuprofen is **not solved at 100 iterations** while its (S) form is (the stock holds the (S) ester); sildenafil is solved in three steps; strychnine returns no reaction at all (a tree of seven nodes that is the bare target). A solved route here means every leaf is in a 2020 ZINC snapshot.

### 0d. The route validator (`benchmarks/synthesis/route_validator.py`, 16 tests, 16 of 16 mutations killed)

Separate outcomes, never one score: molecules parse; reactions parse **and their sides are the molecule nodes they connect** (a consistency check the first plan did not have, added because the planner's route is two parallel representations); mapping `complete` / `partial` / `absent`; **atom conservation only where the mapping is complete, otherwise `not_assessable`**; stock `all_in_stock` / `some_not_in_stock` / `not_provided`; and a list of what the planner did not supply. The conservation rule is fixed: every mapped atom of the product side has exactly one same-element partner on the reactant side; unpartnered reactant atoms are permitted and counted; charge and hydrogen counts are not compared. Nothing is repaired or invented. On the **60 reaction-bearing routes in the fixtures every one validates**, which says the planner's own bookkeeping is consistent (not that the chemistry is right); the checks are shown able to fail by the synthetic mutations (a parse failure, a sides-and-nodes mismatch, a flipped stereocentre, a stripped or partial mapping, a changed element, a missing partner, a missing stock flag).

### 0e. The quality protocol: what the reading changes, and what is proposed

Read from the PaRoutes paper (Genheden and Bjerrum, Digital Discovery 2022, 1, 527; DOI 10.1039/D2DD00015F; in the library as `genheden2022_paroutes.pdf`):

* **Route sets.** Two sets of **10,000 reference routes** from USPTO, set-n1 (one route per patent) and set-n5 (up to five), at most 10 reactions deep, chosen as the most diverse and **checked so that no route has a leaf that is an intermediate in another**. **The stock is the leaves of the reference routes** (13,633 and 13,783 molecules), by design: a ZINC-sized stock "could mask subtle differences between search algorithms" and its snapshot is arbitrary.
* **Metrics proposed there:** solved targets; **top-1, top-5 and top-10 route accuracy by tree edit distance** to the reference route (ranked by a recursive route score with arbitrary reaction costs, so ties are common); and a diversity count. The paper shows why accuracy is a harsh measure: **for about 45% of targets the top-1 route is shorter than the reference, and the leaf overlap is 0.63 to 0.68 while the exact route is recovered for only about 1 in 5**, so a miss is often an alternative route, not a wrong one.
* **Their numbers (the benchmark's own one-step model, trained on USPTO with the reference reactions removed; 500 iterations, no time limit, no filter, single CPU):** MCTS on set-n1 solved **9,714 of 10,000**, search time 303 s and first solution 8.6 s on average, **top-1 0.20, top-5 0.55, top-10 0.61**; on set-n5 solved 9,676, top-1 0.09, top-10 0.42.
* **The contamination finding.** The paper states that a one-step model "trained on the entirety of the USPTO dataset ... was trained on reactions found in the reference routes, making it biased", and using it is "a mix of neural network recommendations and what amounts to literature-lookup". **The public ONNX models AiZynthFinder installs are described only as the USPTO models, and no record says the reference reactions were excluded**, so on PaRoutes targets they are probably in the contaminated class. Consequences, all for the preregistration: (1) a high recovery rate with the public model **is not evidence that the planner generalises**; it is evidence it reproduces patent chemistry it was trained on; (2) a rate **below** the published 0.61 would be a red flag; (3) the claim that can be made is "returns routes that include known patent routes at a literature-consistent rate", never "proposes feasible routes for new targets"; (4) a clean measurement needs either the PaRoutes-excluded model (`uspto_keras_model.hdf5` in Zenodo 7341155, 275 MB, **Keras, so TensorFlow and the NumPy conflict**, and whether it excludes the reference reactions is not stated on the record) or a reference set that is not USPTO.
* **Search budget and noise.** The paper's budget is 500 iterations with no time limit; the spike's searches at 100 iterations took 2 to 16 s, so **500 iterations is affordable** (an hour or two for a few hundred targets in one warm process). Run-to-run variation is real in tree size and route count and absent in the solved flag and top score on the targets tried.

**Proposed protocol and thresholds, for Alex's approval before anything is frozen** (the same pattern as proton affinity and bond dissociation):

* **Targets:** a fixed random draw (seed written in the preregistration) of **300 targets from set-n1**; **stock: the set-n1 stock (the reference leaves)**, loaded as a text stock; **500 iterations, `time_limit` 1,800 s as a safety net only**, `max_transforms` 6, the USPTO policy with the filter, the public ONNX models; one warm process per run.
* **Claim 1, a route was returned (solved with the set stock):** PASS at **at least 0.90** of targets, PARTIAL 0.80 to 0.90, NO-GO below 0.80. (Published 0.971 with the benchmark's own model.)
* **Claim 2, the reference route is among the top 10 returned routes** (exact reaction-set match after removing atom maps, stereo kept; "top" by the planner's own order): PASS at **at least 0.50**, PARTIAL 0.35 to 0.50, NO-GO below 0.35. (Published 0.61 by tree edit distance with an uncontaminated model; the stricter exact-match rule is expected to read lower and a contaminated model higher; **the result is reported with both caveats and never described as generalisation**.)
* **Validity:** among the returned top-1 routes, the fraction **fully validated by the validator** (outcomes 1 to 4) at **at least 0.95** (PASS), PARTIAL down to 0.80, NO-GO below; routes that are `not_assessable` are counted separately and never as valid.
* **Stability:** 20 targets drawn before the run, each run three times; the **solved flag must be identical in at least 19 of 20**; route count and first-solution time are reported as the noise floor.
* **Claim 3, feasibility:** not measured, never reported.
* **Verdict:** GO needs all four PASS; any NO-GO is a NO-GO; otherwise PARTIAL with what works named and Alex's decision. **Resources are reported, not graded** (peak memory 5.3 GB, 1.7 GB on disk, about 16 s to start) but they are part of what a user is told before installing.
* **Why these numbers:** 0.90 sits below the published 0.971 by the margin a different model, a text stock and a Windows build might cost; 0.50 sits 0.11 below the published 0.61 for the stricter match; the validity and stability bars are set at what the fixtures already show (60 of 60 routes valid and a stable solved flag).
* **Not run until Alex says so:** 300 targets at 500 iterations is hours of CPU. It also needs the set-n1 route file, targets and stock from Zenodo 7341155 (about 65 MB), **which has not been downloaded**.
